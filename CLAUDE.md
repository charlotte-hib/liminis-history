# Liminis

Spring Boot 4 + Kotlin backend (`backend/`), Next.js 16 frontend (`frontend/`, see `frontend/CLAUDE.md`). Product and design context: `README.md`.

## Local commands

- Java 25 (jenv, `.java-version`) and Node 24 (nvm, `.nvmrc`). The shell's default Node is older: run `source ~/.nvm/nvm.sh && nvm use` in `frontend/` before any npm command.
- Backend: `cd backend && ./gradlew test` / `./gradlew bootRun` (port 8080). Both need Docker Desktop running: the tests start Postgres with Testcontainers, `bootRun` starts the `db` service of `backend/compose.yaml` (Spring Boot's Docker Compose support).
- Workbench (`backend/workbench`, the curators' tools, on the curator's computer, or from its image in the review stack below): `./gradlew :workbench:bootRun` starts it as a server on `127.0.0.1:8090` (API `api/workbench.yaml`, every request needs an access token from `auth` with its endpoint's scope, and a `Host` from `app.web.allowed-hosts`: `localhost` or `127.0.0.1`, and `workbench` in the review stack); `backend/workbench/wb` (bash, curl, python3) signs in to `auth` as the `wb` client and starts its runs, one at a time, and follows them. `wb draft <id>` drafts a moment's story with Claude from the dataset's intros and proposes its cards as the moment's `STORY` claim and its hook and world around as its `MOMENT_PAGE` claim, its cost in the run's result (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; curate's moment pages start the same run); `wb import` fetches Wikidata's people, their places and occupations, three levels of classes above the occupations, and the intros of their English and French Wikipedia articles into its schema `raw` (its own Flyway migrations, `db/raw`, never dropped), resuming a run that stopped, and refreshes by revision when run again; `wb map` maps the last finished import to rows in schema `dataset` (`db/dataset`, rebuilt on every run): people, their Wikipedia articles, their places with the country each lies in today (Natural Earth's borders, downloaded on the first run into `backend/natural-earth/` and pinned by sha256 in `application.yaml`), and occupations; `wb export` writes those rows to the fetched dataset's files (`backend/dataset-export/`, ignored), pushes them with ORAS from its Docker image to the private package `ghcr.io/charlotte-hib/liminis-history-dataset` (with `gh auth token`, which needs `write:packages`), and pins its digest in the data repository's `data/fetched.lock`; `wb claims` proposes the facts of the people of the release's moments as claims to review, in schema `curation` (`db/curation`, never dropped: the curator's decisions), and `wb occupations` a domain to choose for each occupation held; `wb connections` imports, once, the connections written by hand in the data repository's `data/connections.jsonl` on `main` as approved `CONNECTION` claims (people by Q-id: the data repository's `people.jsonl` needs its Q-ids first), lines already claimed left as they are; `wb sample` writes `sample/dataset/`, the public sample's slice of the last export; `wb translate` drafts in French with Claude every record of the data repository's `main` with no French yet, or whose English changed (updating the French approved before), as `TRANSLATION` claims, reviewed under curate's Français tabs, published beside the English while it still matches (else the French before stays on the site, and curate says it is behind); approving a new header, story, anecdote or connection of a translated record starts its French's update; `wb queues` shows what waits. A claim has at most one holder (`claim.held_by`, by username): only they or the admin write it, and writing one nobody holds takes it (`ClaimRules.mayWrite`; `PUT`/`DELETE /api/claims/{id}/holder`, `/api/moments/{id}/holder`, the admin's `DELETE /api/holders/{username}`). curate's moment pages (`/moments`) show everyone alive in each moment (fetched people tied to its region by any place, alive during it) with their claims, and lead to its header (hook, world around and the moment's painting), edited and approved at `/moments/<id>/header` under its preview (with no claim yet, from the release's: `POST /api/moments/{id}/page`), and its story, reviewed at `/moments/<id>/story` beside a preview of each card (`GET /api/moments/{id}/preview`), each card's subject chosen by search (`GET /api/moments/{id}/subjects`) and its painting, like the moment's, on Wikimedia Commons (`GET /api/paintings`, CC0 or public domain only, never public domain in the United States only) or at another address. Publishing (curate's Publish page, `POST /api/publication`) writes `data/occupations.jsonl`, `data/corrections.jsonl`, `data/anecdotes.jsonl` and `data/connections.jsonl` whole from the approved claims (an anecdote or a connection naming a fetched person held back while `app.publish.fetched-people` is false; `connections.jsonl` left as it is while a line of it on `main` has no claim, until `wb connections`), and each approved header's hook, world and painting and each approved story's cards with theirs into its `data/moments/<id>.json` (the rest of the file untouched; a story showing a fetched person is held back while `app.publish.fetched-people` is false), and opens a pull request on the data repository through the workbench's GitHub App (`app.publish.github.client-id`, key at `~/.config/liminis/github-app.pem`, PKCS#8), after a passkey used in the last 5 minutes. Its Postgres is `compose.workbench.yaml` (project `liminis-workbench`, volume `workbench-db-data`), started the same way and never stopped by it, as `auth` shares it: `docker compose -f compose.workbench.yaml stop`.
- auth (`backend/auth`, the curators' sign-in, on the curator's computer, or from its image in the review stack below): `./gradlew :auth:bootRun` on `localhost:9000` (always `localhost`: passkeys are bound to it), Spring Authorization Server with passkeys, its tables in schema `auth` of the workbench's Postgres (`db/auth`, never dropped; it starts that Postgres if needed, never stops it). Accounts in Spring Security's `users`/`authorities` (`JdbcUserDetailsManager`): the admin (`curator`, `ROLE_ADMIN`) and the curators the admin invites (`ROLE_CURATOR`); a token's scopes are those curate asked for that the role allows (`Role`), read again at every refresh; the accounts API (`api/auth.yaml`, `/api/accounts`, scope `admin`, audience `auth`, a passkey of the last 5 minutes to invite or revoke) invites with a one-time link valid 7 days and revokes (passkeys, links and sessions deleted, the account kept, its tokens refused by the workbench at once: it reads `auth.users.enabled` for each token's subject). Clients `curate` (authorization code with PKCE after a passkey, refresh tokens used once, 8-hour session; scopes `curation:read`, `curation:write`, `publish`, `admin`) and `wb` (client credentials; `curation:read`, `runs`), registered from `Clients.kt` on every start, their secrets written to `~/.config/liminis/{curate,wb}-client-secret`. Access tokens: ES256 JWTs for the audience `workbench`, 10 minutes; signing keys in `auth.signing_key`, a new one every 30 days, the old one published 30 days more. Access tokens carry `auth_time`; a request's `max_age` asks for the passkey again when it is older. With no passkey registered for the admin, or with `--args=--app.auth.one-time-link=true`, it logs a one-time link that registers one.
- curate (`curate/`, the curators' review app, on the curator's computer, or from its image in the review stack below): `cd curate && npm run dev`, opened at `http://localhost:3100`, with `auth` and the workbench running. Its server signs curators in through `auth` (`openid-client`), keeps the tokens in an encrypted cookie (`jose`) and calls the workbench API with them, and `auth`'s accounts API for the admin's Team page (`/team`); the browser never sees a token. A person's facts are reviewed on the People pages: `/people` lists everyone fetched (and anyone with a facts claim the dataset no longer holds), or with a moment everyone alive in it, the best known first, by filter and name (`GET /api/people/review`); `/people/<qid>` edits their facts beside a preview of the site's person panel (`GET /api/people/{qid}/preview`: its life rows copied from the site's `LifeInTime`, keep them alike), adds an anecdote, edits, removes and adds their connections (`GET /api/people/{qid}/connections`; a new one is a waiting claim at once), approves the facts and the connections changed or waiting on the page in one transaction (`POST /api/decisions`, undone together by `POST /api/decisions/undo`), and moves through the same list (`GET /api/people/{qid}/neighbours`); a person nobody proposed yet gets their claim there first. Its types come from `api/workbench.yaml` and `api/auth.yaml` (`npm run api:types`, committed, CI fails when stale); CI runs its lint and build (not a required check). Its e2e tests (`e2e/curate/`, `AUTH_LOG=<auth's output> npx playwright test --config curate.config.ts`, `CURATE_URL`) sign in with a passkey from `auth`'s one-time link and decide on claims: run them against `auth` (started with the one-time link) and a workbench on a throwaway copy of its database, with curate's `LIMINIS_SECRETS_DIR` pointing at that `auth`'s secrets, never in CI.
- Review stack (`compose.review.yaml`, project `liminis-review`, for the review on the server): curate, auth and the workbench from their images (`curate/Dockerfile`; `backend/Dockerfile`'s targets `workbench` and `auth`, the site's being the default), one user id (10001) in all three, and a Postgres of their own (the workbench's, volume `review-db-data`, password from `review-db.env`, untracked). Set from outside: `AUTH_URL` (auth's issuer, the name passkeys are bound to) and `CURATE_URL`; host names allowed in `app.web.allowed-hosts` (workbench) and `app.auth.allowed-hosts` (auth, plus its issuer's); the workbench fetches auth's keys at `http://auth:9000` and curate calls auth there too (`AUTH_INTERNAL_URL`); keys as Compose secrets, files in `/run/secrets` (`anthropic-api-key`, read through Spring's `configtree`; `github-app.pem`), from `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY_FILE` and `GITHUB_APP_KEY_FILE` (default `review-secrets/`, untracked); the client secrets on the volume `secrets`, shared by auth and curate. The workbench reads `sample/` as its release, as `bootRun` does. Every port on loopback (`AUTH_PORT`, `WORKBENCH_PORT`, `CURATE_PORT`); behind a proxy, auth takes the visitor's address and HTTPS from `X-Forwarded-*`. auth and curate limit requests per visitor (Bucket4j in `RateLimit.kt`; curate's copy of the site's `lib/rate-limit.ts`), never from loopback or private addresses. Locally: the commands in the file's header; curate's e2e run against it with `CURATE_URL`. CI builds, scans and pushes the three images (`ghcr.io/charlotte-hib/liminis-history-{workbench,auth,curate}`, public) with the site's, and the deploy updates the stack on the server (below). On the server, curate and auth are public by name behind Caddy (`deploy/Caddyfile`'s `liminis-curate` and `liminis-auth`: security headers, `noindex`, a `robots.txt` that disallows everything, and for auth only the paths a browser needs); the workbench and the database are not. `wb` runs there through `deploy/wb` (it reads the wb secret from the stack's volume): on the curator's computer, `WB_SERVER=deploy@<server> wb <command>` runs it over Tailscale SSH, and `wb export` exports there, copies the files back (`deploy/wb export-files`) and pushes them from the computer, with gh's token. `deploy/backup-review.sh` (`BACKUP_SERVER=...`) pulls a pg_dump of the stack's database to `~/liminis-backups`, keeping 7, nightly from launchd on the curator's computer; it holds the decisions and auth's signing keys. To turn the review on for a server: DNS for its two names; their blocks in the server's Caddyfile (`import liminis-curate`, `import liminis-auth`); `review.env`; `review-secrets/` with the two keys; `curl` and `python3` (for `wb`); the database restored from a dump (one-off `postgres` container on `liminis-review_default`, `pg_restore --no-owner`); then the first passkey from the one-time link in auth's log (`docker compose -f compose.review.yaml --env-file review.env logs auth`), registered with no passkey left for the admin.
- Database: Postgres 18 (the `db` service in `docker-compose.yml`, image pinned by digest; the tests read their image from it). Schema in SQL migrations, `backend/core/src/main/resources/db/migration`, run by Flyway; Hibernate only validates (`ddl-auto: validate`). Each release is loaded into its own schema, `release_<commit[:12]>` (`APP_RELEASE_ID` from `RELEASE_SHA`), by the last migration, `LoadRelease` (version 999); `release_local` (no id: bootRun, tests, CI) is rebuilt on every start. Never edit an applied migration: add one. Roles: `site_owner` (Flyway, owns the schemas), `site_reader` (the API: select, plus update of person's Wikipedia columns), from `db/roles.sql`.
- Frontend: `cd frontend && npm run lint && npm run build` / `npm run dev` (port 3000). English at `/`, French under `/fr` (next-intl: `i18n/`, pages under `app/[locale]`, links and router from `@/i18n/navigation`); every interface string goes in both `messages/en.json` and `messages/fr.json`, years and ages through `lib/years.ts`'s `useYears`/`yearWords`; the release's text comes from the API in the page's language (`lang`), each record saying its own `lang`, set on its text.
- API contract: `api/openapi.yaml`, spec first. The backend generates its controller interfaces and models from it (`./gradlew :site:openApiGenerate`, run by every build): change the spec, never the generated code. The frontend's types: `npm run api:types` in `frontend/` regenerates `lib/api-schema.ts` (committed; CI fails when stale), and `lib/api.ts` and `lib/api-browser.ts` call the backend through `openapi-fetch`. Lint: `npx @redocly/cli@2.54.2 lint api/openapi.yaml api/workbench.yaml api/auth.yaml --config api/redocly.yaml`. auth's accounts API, `api/auth.yaml`, generates its interface the same way (`:auth:openApiGenerate`). The workbench's contract, `api/workbench.yaml`, generates its interfaces the same way (`:workbench:openApiGenerate`). Swagger UI on `bootRun` only: http://localhost:8080/swagger-ui.html.
- API tests: with the backend running (`--app.wikipedia.enrich=false`), `ijhttp --env-file http/http-client.env.json --env local http/api.http` (add `-V baseUrl=http://localhost:<port>` for another port). Never point them at production: only `http/production.http` (read-only GETs, no visitor events) runs there, by `.github/workflows/smoke.yml`.
- Both in Docker: `docker compose up --build`, then http://localhost:3000. Docker Desktop is often not running on this Mac.
- End-to-end (Playwright, against a running site): `cd e2e && npm ci && npm run typecheck && npx playwright test` (`BASE_URL`, default http://localhost:3000). Locally it drives the installed Google Chrome: Playwright's Chromium does not run on macOS 13.

## Production

Merging to `main` deploys. Server details and access are kept out of this public repo: @~/.claude/liminis-ops.md

- **Pipeline** (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`): pull requests run backend tests and frontend lint and build, then the `images` job builds each image once (a one-day artifact) and the API tests (`http/`), end-to-end tests (`e2e/`) and vulnerability scan (`scan.yml`) load those exact images; the first two and the scan are required checks. The scan (Grype, `anchore/scan-action`) reports every finding to the Security tab and fails only on a critical CVE with a fixed version; ignoring one goes in `.grype.yaml`, with a reason and an expiry. It also runs daily at 06:43 UTC on the images of the last successful production deployment. On `main`, the same images are pushed to GHCR (`ghcr.io/charlotte-hib/liminis-history-{backend,frontend}`, tagged `sha-<commit>`) with signed SLSA provenance and SPDX SBOM attestations. The `deploy` job (environment `production`, `main` only) waits for every test and the scan, verifies the provenance, joins the tailnet through Tailscale workload identity federation, and runs `deploy/deploy.sh` over Tailscale SSH. Then `smoke.yml` checks the live site from outside (also daily at 06:17 UTC, address from the repository variable `SITE_URL`); its failure turns the run red but never rolls back. `workflow_dispatch` redeploys `main` by hand.
- **No secrets in GitHub.** Repository variables only. Do not add SSH keys or tokens.
- **Releases**: production serves the private repo `charlotte-hib/liminis-history-data` (`data/`, cloned at `~/Desktop/liminis-history-data`), not `sample/`. Its CI starts the public images of the commit in production on the candidate `data/` and probes them; on merge it pushes `data/` as a private OCI artifact, `ghcr.io/charlotte-hib/liminis-history-release:<data/'s Git tree hash>` (newest 10 kept), and prints the two lines for `release.lock`. Changing `release.lock` is a pull request of its own here. The package grants this repo's Actions read access; the deploy job pulls it with ORAS and pipes it to `deploy.sh`. A format change lands here first (with `sample/`), then in the data repo. Never copy `data/` into this repo.
- **`deploy/deploy.sh`** runs on the server in a clone of this repo: checks out the commit, fills in `db.env` and `db-roles.env` with random passwords where missing, unpacks the release bundle from stdin into `releases/<RELEASE_ID>` (kept: the three most recently deployed, and the current and previous ones), writes the release (commit, image digests and `RELEASE_DIR`) to `.env`, `docker compose up` for `backend` and `frontend`, probes `/` and `/api/search?q=a` (an API path the frontend forwards) through the reverse proxy, and rolls back to `.env.previous` if unhealthy within 3 minutes. Once healthy, it starts or updates Prometheus and Grafana when `grafana.env` exists (adding `COMPOSE_PROFILES=monitoring` to `.env`); a monitoring failure never fails the deploy or rolls it back. Then, when the server has `review.env` (`AUTH_URL`, `CURATE_URL`, no trailing slash), it moves the review stack (project `liminis-review`) to the commit's three images (`review-images.env`), unless the workbench is running a run, probes both names through Caddy, and if unhealthy rolls the review alone back to `review-images.env.previous` and fails the deploy (the site's release stands); it makes `review-db.env` when missing and keeps `review-secrets/` (mode 700, its files 644, for the containers' user 10001: `anthropic-api-key`, `github-app.pem`). Its body is in functions because it rewrites itself through `git checkout`.
- **Compose**: `backend` (no published port, `SPRING_PROFILES_ACTIVE=prod` points it at `db` and moves Actuator, `/actuator/health` and `/actuator/prometheus`, to port 8081, reachable on the Compose network only), `db` (Postgres, no published port, volume `db-data`, 256 MB), `db-roles` (one-shot: creates the roles and sets their passwords on every `up`; `backend` waits for it) and `frontend` on `127.0.0.1:3000`. The release is `RELEASE_DIR` (default `sample/`), mounted read-only at `/app/release` (the image holds no data); the backend keeps the current release schema and the two newest others. Wikipedia enrichment takes about a minute on a new schema. Passwords: `db.env` (`POSTGRES_PASSWORD`, superuser: `db` and `db-roles` only) and `db-roles.env` (`SITE_OWNER_PASSWORD`, `SITE_READER_PASSWORD`: `db-roles` and `backend`), both untracked, next to `.env`, mode 600, made by `deploy.sh`; locally `db/defaults.env` and `db/roles.env` apply. Postgres only reads the superuser's password when it creates its data directory: if `db.env` is ever lost, remove the `db-data` volume too (nothing in it needs keeping). Change a role's password in `db-roles.env`, then redeploy.
- **Monitoring** (`monitoring/`, Compose profile `monitoring`, off in CI and by default): `prometheus` (30 days or 1 GB) and `grafana` on `127.0.0.1:3001` only, never in the Caddy snippet, with named volumes `prometheus-data` and `grafana-data`. Grafana's admin password comes from `grafana.env` (`GF_SECURITY_ADMIN_PASSWORD=...`, untracked, next to `.env`), which can also override `monitoring/grafana/defaults.env` (e.g. `GF_SERVER_ROOT_URL`); keep overridable settings there, not under `environment:`, which wins over both. Datasource, dashboards and alerting are provisioned from the repo: change the JSON in `monitoring/grafana/dashboards/` and the YAML in `monitoring/grafana/provisioning/`, not in the UI. One alert: API traffic above 1 request a second for 10 minutes, emailed to `ALERT_EMAIL` through the `GF_SMTP_*` settings in `grafana.env`; `defaults.env` holds a placeholder address so Grafana starts without them. Visitor events (`POST /api/events`, `frontend/lib/analytics.ts`, `VisitorEvents.kt`) carry only labels from closed sets: no ids, IPs, user agents or URLs.
- **Rate limit** (`frontend/proxy.ts`, `frontend/lib/rate-limit.ts`): per visitor, keyed on the `X-Forwarded-For` Caddy sets (IPv6 by /64), in the frontend's memory. Requests without it and from private addresses are never limited, so CI, local runs and the deploy probe are not; keep Caddy in front of the frontend.
- Keep server details (addresses, hostnames, users, firewall, other sites) and personal details out of tracked files and commit messages, including `README.md` and this file. The server provides the site addresses: its Caddyfile lists them, and `site.env` (untracked, next to `.env`) gives `deploy.sh` the address to probe.
